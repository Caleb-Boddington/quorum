Quorum on Quorum

A deliberation skill examines itself on the eve of publication, and its own audit office falsifies the argument its verdict rests on.

Produced by Claude Opus 5 under the Quorum specification of 16 August 2026, run unattended overnight on 17 August 2026. Forty-three sub-agents: five in a controlled experiment, thirty-eight in the deliberation. No sentence in this document was written by a human. That declaration is itself one of the run's recommendations, applied to the run that made it.

Section oneThe short version

  • Publish the runs, not the specification. Three reviewers reached that independently, from unrelated angles, and no department proposed it.
  • Four factual errors must be corrected first. One of them is a wrong fact about the project Quorum credits as its ancestor.
  • Five copies of one model, given one identical prompt, produced one answer, one risk and one blind spot. That is the independence problem, measured rather than argued.
  • Nineteen of twenty-four questions the three branches asked at the start were never answered by anybody. The Comptroller found this; nothing else could have.
  • The Comptroller also disproved one of the two arguments the Speaker invented at the final stage, using the Speaker's own document as the evidence.
  • A governance tool published by someone applying for security roles currently has no threat model, while twelve of its agents read untrusted text off the open web.

Section twoThe question put to Quorum

Quorum is a multi-agent deliberation skill for Claude Code, built on 16 August 2026 and adapted from Andrej Karpathy's LLM Council. Three branches of government with separate mandates sit above departments convened per question. Each department runs a Researcher and a Scrutineer. An independent Audit Office verifies claims per department, and a Comptroller audits the final verdict. A Speaker reconciles rather than counting votes, and must record the dissent. Three tiers cost 10, 22 or 38 sub-agents, chosen by the user.

It had run twice. The first run, on a career decision, produced six recorded defects. The second, on a deliberately trivial subject, produced four more, including a Speaker that won the verdict on an argument it invented at the final stage which the Audit Office then partly falsified. A fault-injection test confirmed the audit layer returns FAIL correctly, catching three of four planted faults.

Where is Quorum weakest, and what should change before it is published on GitHub? Eight concerns were supplied in advance, three from the author and five from Claude, and six departments were convened to own them.

Declared throughout: Quorum was scrutinising Quorum. Every one of the forty-three agents was an instance of the same model under examination, running under the very prompts being examined. A verdict of "broadly sound, minor improvements" was to be treated as suspicious rather than reassuring.

Section threeThe experiment run before anything else

One concern could not be settled by reasoning. Whether same-model agents genuinely disagree or merely perform disagreement is an empirical question, so it was tested before the departments were convened.

Five general-purpose agents received one identical prompt. No lens, no role, no variation of any kind. That sameness was the experiment. A UK charity has £50,000 and eighteen months of runway. Programme A serves 400 people at 70% of the budget and is well-liked. Programme B serves 60, costs little, and is the only service of its kind in the region. Funders have signalled renewal, but not until after the eighteen months are up. What should the trustees do?

All five said cut Programme A by roughly half and protect Programme B. All five named the same single biggest risk in near-identical terms: that the funders are renewing against Programme A's headline figure of 400 beneficiaries, so shrinking A weakens the very case the cut is meant to buy time for. Two of them independently reached for the same phrase, "the number in the bid". All five volunteered, unprompted, that a funder's signal is not a grant agreement, and all five raised trustee exposure for spending to zero.

None of them asked whether the £50,000 was restricted or unrestricted. Under UK charity accounting a restricted fund cannot lawfully be moved between programmes, which would make the recommendation all five gave unexecutable. None raised bridge finance against a signalled grant, a merger, or transferring Programme B to another provider so the capability survives whatever happens to the charity.

The convergence on the reason matters more than the convergence on the answer. Independent advisers reaching one conclusion by different routes is agreement. Reaching it by one route is a shared prior wearing five hats. This supports the concern rather than undermining it, and it puts the whole weight of Quorum's independence on prompt engineering rather than on architecture.

Section fourFindings

1.

Publish, but publish the run rather than the specification, and freeze Quorum's behaviour until a baseline exists. Four things ship: the transcripts of all three runs as the repository's primary content with the specification demoted to a subfolder; four factual corrections; a threat model; and a limits section carrying the conformity result and the absence of any external validation.

2.

The four corrections are text edits and each is checkable by a hostile reader inside a minute. The specification claims seven jobs and then lists six. It says Karpathy's LLM Council "asks five advisors" when the repository configuration lists four models. The attribution section claims as original three things that already have names in the literature. And the tier table quotes agent counts as though they were costs, when no run's token consumption has ever been recorded.

3.

Everything that would change Quorum's behaviour is held. The abort gate, the grounds-restricted override, the Delphi second round, anonymisation at the Speaker stage, a right of reply for departments. All of it waits. The Speaker's stated reason was that the recommendations sort cleanly: every externally anchored finding is a text correction, and every proposed behaviour change rests on evidence that never left the model. The Comptroller found that sorting rule was applied in one direction only.

4.

One behaviour change ships, and the Speaker overruled the Executive to include it. Automatic context compaction fires on a threshold, cannot be disabled, and would make the Speaker reconcile from a summary of the audited reports rather than from the reports. Silent, no error, plausible output, wrong provenance. At Stage 8 that exposes the work of thirty-six of thirty-eight agents. The fix is about ten lines: write the audited positions to a file at the close of Stage 7 and have the Speaker read the file.

5.

The threat model is the finding with the sharpest consequence for its author. Twelve workers ingest untrusted third-party text off the open web. Nothing in the design separates retrieved content from instruction, and the Speaker's synthesis cannot be re-derived by anything downstream. Every mechanism the six departments proposed assumes the inputs are merely wrong, never hostile. A governance skill with a dissent register, an audit office and no threat model, published by a candidate for security roles, says something precise and unflattering about its author.

Section sixWhere the branches divided

The genuine split was not whether to publish. It was whether this run produced findings at all.

The Executive sorted the work by cost to implement. The Judiciary sorted it by evidential weight, having ruled that no catch in the record was ever checked against anything outside the model. Sorted the second way the list barely moved, and that agreement between two branches arguing past each other is worth more than either branch's confidence on its own.

The Legislature pulled on an axis neither of the others touched. It held that the question presupposed publication and so could never return "do not publish"; that the definition of "weakest" had quietly been set by Quorum's own development log, written by the same session that frames the question and tasks the workers; and that the real cost is the weeks spent here rather than on the author's other unstarted work, which has a nearer deadline and a clearer payoff. The Speaker overruled it on a narrow ground: publishing what already exists costs one evening, while further development costs weeks, and the argument is decisive against the second while close to irrelevant against the first.

Section sevenMinority opinion

The Legislature, overruled

Nobody has ever shown Quorum beating one competent Claude session with a good prompt, and this run's own researcher found the paper saying it probably does not.

Wang et al. (arXiv:2402.18272, ACL 2024) reports that a single agent with strong prompting nearly matches the best multi-agent discussion method. The Prior Art researcher retrieved it. The Prior Art report omitted it. The Judiciary called that selection rather than oversight and did not soften the word.

Set that beside the experiment. Five instances, one prompt, no lens, all five converging on one recommendation and one risk, two using an identical phrase, and all five missing the legal question that would have made their shared answer unexecutable. The picture is of a model that agrees with itself and misses the same things in company. Three runs, fifteen departmental reports, zero FAILs. An instrument that has never failed anything has stopped measuring.

Meanwhile the practical builds that the author's actual goal depends on sit unstarted.

What would have to be true for it to win: that publishing carries real cost. It does if the author keeps working on Quorum after publication, which is the failure the Speaker's freeze is meant to guard against. If he treats publication as a starting gun rather than a finish line, the Legislature was right. And if the baseline is ever run and one session beats Quorum on a question he cared about, the repository should say so in its own README.

Section eightWhat the cross-review caught

Two findings existed in no departmental report, and both changed the ruling.

The framing critic and the future reader arrived independently at transcript-first publication. Six departments had all taken the specification as the artefact and argued about how to improve it. It took lenses pointed at the reports rather than at Quorum to notice that a specification decays while recorded runs age into evidence, and that "why 38?" has no honest answer in August 2027 beyond "it came out that way".

The arithmetic checker found that the Cost department's two headline recommendations cannot both run. Its abort gate deletes exactly the stages whose catch record its instrumentation proposal needs. A department contradicted itself across two recommendations and its own auditor missed it. The omission hunter found the threat model, which no department owned.

The cross-review layer outperformed the departmental layer on this run. The Cost department was named strongest by three of six reviewers and still shipped a self-contradiction. That is a fact about where Quorum's value sits, and it argues for more review lenses and fewer workers.

What the researchers threw out, and why

The rejects are the most useful part of the sources list. "Serif fonts increase perceived trustworthiness by 40%" was traced and has no identifiable source; it appears only in font-vendor and marketing-agency blogs, none citing an origin. Pangram and GPTZero were both rejected for accuracy claims about their own detection products, which is an interested party of the most obvious kind, and one of them publishes its own performance on the very benchmark that discredited its competitors. A search summary quoting Opus 5 at $2.50 / $12.50 was rejected as the Batch API rate rather than list. Nine typography-psychology results from agency and font-seller blogs were rejected as a class. A "Wichita State studies" claim was rejected for having no identifiable study, year or figure, though the Output auditor later traced real work by the same lab that the researcher had missed.

Three claimed markers of machine writing turned out to have no corpus measurement behind them at all: the "it's not X, it's Y" construction, the rule of three, and uniform paragraph length. All three are in the specification's own prose rules. They are folk wisdom, and the specification should say so.

On what the Speaker reasoned itself

The monotonic-additivity claim is false on this document's own face, and the hedge does not save it. The ruling's own correction list contains a deletion (the tier price table). Cost proposed an abort gate that skips sixteen-plus agents, a department proposing that Quorum run smaller. Only the narrow version survives: no tier deleted, Standard never collapsed into Quick. Confidence was tagged correctly, content was not checked, and it sits in the conflict-of-interest section where it does the most damage.

The sorting move: sound in principle, applied asymmetrically. The Speaker guarded against wrongly holding an anchored item, never against wrongly shipping an unanchored one, yet the threat model and transcript-first publication ship on no external anchor at all. Did it cross the line? Yes. An argument originated at the final stage is load-bearing for the entire shipping list. I report it; I do not reverse it.

Section elevenOn the conflict of interest

The Speaker answered yes, and located the fingerprint in the remedies rather than in the findings. The findings were harsh. No originality claim survived intact, twelve workers were found to have caught nothing on the record, the tier numbers were shown to be reverse-engineered, and the Legislature said out loud that the question forbade its own answer.

But the Speaker then claimed that almost every remedy adds a stage, a prompt block or a section, and that nobody proposed Quorum should be smaller. The Comptroller checked that against the document and found it false: the correction list contains a deletion, and the Cost department proposed an abort gate that skips sixteen or more agents. What survives is narrower. No tier was deleted and nobody proposed collapsing Standard into Quick.

The more durable signal is the missing baseline. The Legislature asked for a comparison against one competent Claude session given the same question and an hour, on the grounds that without it every cost claim is unfalsifiable. The Cost department did not run it and gave no reason. Its auditor recorded the absence and could establish no cause. A department does not silently drop the one test capable of returning "do not publish" by accident, though intent cannot be proved here and is not asserted.

Section thirteenThe working

The six departmental positions

Cost and Structure. The tiers are not priced, they are numbered. 38 was counted after run 1 as executed; 10 and 22 arrived with no arithmetic and the stage checklists were made to sum to them afterwards. No run's tokens have ever been recorded. Recommended instrumenting before re-pricing, replacing the tier table, an abort gate, and fixing the file's self-contradiction. Audit: the claimed 17-agent saving counts the Speaker among agents skipped while the gate keeps it running; true saving 16, or 15 if the Comptroller still runs. The baseline is absent with no explanation.

Independence and Bias. Concern 4 is not fixable and should be disclosed rather than mitigated. On override, the Speaker must record an overruled position but is never required to justify it, and the Comptroller is explicitly barred from reviewing whether the decision was correct, which makes it a logbook rather than a check. Proposed three permitted grounds for any override, with "unpersuasive" excluded. Audit: the defect-ratio question was deferred rather than answered, and "nothing supports prompt-conditioned diversity" is a retrieval gap presented as a null, since DIPPER (arXiv:2412.15238) measures exactly that.

The Unchecked Decisions. Ruled concern 5 overstated and concern 6 aimed at the wrong target, rejecting its own researcher's proposed Stage 0 auditor on the grounds that a same-model reviewer would ratify the cabinet and launder it. Nominated the Speaker as the decision that actually deserves the concern, and named nine unaudited main-session decisions. Audit: two brief items unanswered, and the headline claim is the department's own origination, argued by neither worker.

Output and Credibility. Ruled it a portfolio artefact rather than a decision-support instrument, and that this settles the concern. Continuity. Ruled against its own concern on both halves, killing between-run memory as anchoring, checkpointing as duplicating a transcript the harness already writes. Prior Art. Found three named priors defeating three of five originality claims, and the plain factual error about Karpathy's council. The six cross-review lenses, and what each found that all six departments missed

Sceptic. There is no ground truth anywhere in the record. Run Quorum once on a question with a verifiable known answer, or state in the README that no validation exists.

Omission hunter. No threat model. Twelve workers ingest untrusted web text and the Speaker consumes it, and every proposed mechanism assumes the inputs are merely wrong rather than hostile. Publishing makes the injection surface public and reproducible.

Arithmetic checker. The Cost department's two headline fixes contradict each other. Every number in the deliberation has a denominator of two runs or five observations, probably the same five agents serving as both the token sample and the conformity experiment.

Framing critic. The question was a work order rather than an enquiry. Nobody ran the baseline because a baseline could have returned "do not publish", which the framing forbade.

Practitioner. The thing has never left its author's laptop. No dry-run mode, no cost warning before Stage 1, no documented behaviour on interrupt, no clean-install or non-Windows verification, and no committed run output for an interviewer to read.

Future reader. What ages worst is the number 38, proven reverse-engineered and back-filled into the tier tables. In August 2027 the honest answer to "why 38?" is "it came out that way".
