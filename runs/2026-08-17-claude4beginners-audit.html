claude4beginners.co.uk: accuracy, coverage and whether the design reads as made by a person

Accurate, with three things that are wrong rather than stale. Designed, not generated, though the one defect a stranger feels is unfixed. And the completeness standard it was being measured against was never the author's.

Published with redactions, 17 August 2026 Two things are withheld from this copy and both are marked where they were cut: the specific enumeration of an authentication surface whose review is still outstanding, and a personal credential-handling incident belonging to the author rather than to Quorum. No finding, verdict or dissent has been altered, and the Comptroller’s NOT SOUND finding is reproduced in full. The redactions are deliberately visible: a run record that quietly drops its awkward parts is worth less than one that says where it was cut. See ADR-0012.

Tier notice

Full tier, 38 sub-agents: six departments, twelve workers, six auditors, six cross-reviewers under distinct lenses, three branches, a Speaker and a Comptroller. Every checking layer ran and no report was sent back. Three things it did not check, and they should be read as unexamined rather than clean: nobody reviewed the sign-up, sign-in and password-reset code that writes to a live database from the browser; nobody watched a real beginner use the site; and no signed-in path was ever tested.

Section oneThe short version

  • Three statements on the page are wrong, not merely out of date. A privacy claim that is more reassuring than the truth, an unfinishable route for installing a skill from GitHub, and a glossary that breaks its own promise.
  • The design reads as made by a person, but the measure is broken: lines run 95 to 113 characters where the stylesheet intends 72, and that is what a stranger actually feels.
  • The completeness question was answered against the wrong yardstick. One department measured the guide against the vendor's entire shipping surface, which moved on 37 dated days in roughly 100.
  • Six departments prescribed 51.8 to 58.3 hours between them. No department could see that total, because each saw only its own remit.
  • The Comptroller returned NOT SOUND on the verdict and its finding is reproduced below, unedited, including the part where it corrects an error introduced during the run.
  • Nobody in this run examined the authentication code. That is a gate on sending the URL to another employer, not on editing the page.

Section twoThe question

Does claude4beginners.co.uk, as live on 17 August 2026, (a) cover Claude accurately and completely, and (b) present visual and interaction design that reads as a deliberately designed object rather than a generated template, when judged by a stranger arriving cold?

A free beginner's guide to Claude: one self-contained HTML page carrying all its own CSS, 348,810 bytes served, fifteen numbered parts plus About, What has changed and Feedback, all behind hash anchors on one URL. Written by a UK sport media graduate with no professional tech experience moving into cyber security, and doubling as a portfolio piece employers read. 89 unique visitors on 16 August 2026, the only day ever measured. Nobody has been observed using it.

Coverage standard set by the author: Parts 00 to 11 beginner-complete, nothing skipped for being obvious, no jargon unexplained. Parts 12 to 14 power-user-complete, with two suspected gaps named by him: how to get skills from GitHub, and what a repository is. Both were confirmed present.

Section threeFindings

1.

Yes on accuracy, with three exceptions that are wrong rather than stale. Part 02 says that turning off training means past conversations "won't be" used, "including ones you've already had". Anthropic's privacy documentation says the opposite: data already in training stays in models already trained. That is the single decision the guide tells the reader to make deliberately, and it is more reassuring than the truth.

2.

Part 08 tells the reader that GitHub skills are "plain text files: you download one and upload it". The actual route is to zip the folder, then use "+", "+ Create skill", "Upload a skill". A non-developer cannot finish from the text as written. Both this and the privacy line were confirmed by an auditor that fetched the live page independently.

3.

The Part 11 glossary promises "a translation of every word this manual has thrown at you", then omits repository, plugin, Cowork, Git, worktree and CLAUDE.md, all words the guide itself uses. That is a promise the page breaks about itself, so it counts as wrong rather than absent. One correction from the audit: MCP does not belong on that list, because the guide never uses the word at all.

4.

No on completeness, and the standard it was measured against was never the author's. The curriculum department built its own yardstick, declared it unsourced, and produced a 22.5-hour list of missing features by treating the vendor's shipping surface as the standard. That surface moved on 37 dated days in roughly 100. The guide is complete against what it promises the reader. It is not complete against Anthropic's product surface, and it should stop trying to be.

5.

Yes on design, and the reasoning behind that yes was thin. The visual design department concluded "not generated" from 53 spacing literals, 37 size declarations, Fraunces at WONK 1 and an feTurbulence paper grain, none of which a reader ever sees. The conclusion survives anyway, because the template comparators it tested against are genuinely what generated pages look like and this is genuinely not that.

6.

What a cold stranger does feel is line length, and the measure is broken. --measure: 72ch computes to 775px against a 768px box, so the cap never binds, and measured lines run 95 to 113 characters. Three independent instrument readings agree. Do not ship the 62ch value the department prescribed: its own auditor set 62ch on the live page and measured lines up to 100 characters, against the 80-character bound the recommendation cites. Fix it by measuring.

7.

Roughly seven to fifteen hours of work, in two sittings, not the 51.8 to 58.3 the six departments collectively prescribed. Sitting one is text only and carries zero layout risk: the privacy correction, the Part 08 route, the six glossary terms, Cowork defined at its first use in Part 01, the prompt-injection callout duplicated into Parts 08 and 09 (duplicated, never moved, because moving it renumbers four parts), contact links in the footer, and a regenerated social image. Sitting two is mechanical: the 320px grid overflow, the JavaScript-off pager, theme init moved into the head, reduced motion on two smooth scrolls, two contrast tokens, the h4 that is smaller than the body text it heads, and the sign-in modal focus escape.

8.

Defer indefinitely: the date-stamp consolidation, the type-scale rebuild, the contents plate colours and the entire month list. The Comptroller has qualified the seven-hour figure as arithmetic that does not sum, and its correction stands: the ruling's own items at departmental hours total 11.85 to 14.85.

Section fourWhere the branches divided

The Executive and the Legislature disagreed about whether this is a programme or a repair, and the Legislature was closer to right.

The Executive proposed five sequenced slices, each its own zip, each checked live before the next. Correct method, and roughly fifteen hours of ship-now work before diagrams and apparatus even start. The Legislature said the bill is unpayable as written: four certification exams by 6 August 2027, a job search, course instalments falling in September, October and November 2026, and what was actually asked for was assurance, not a work programme. It put the defensible spend under four hours.

The Judiciary took a third axis entirely. It ruled that passing audit establishes only that quoted lines exist as quoted, and that because every department except partly two of them dropped the verifiable-defect versus aesthetic-preference labelling the brief demanded, large parts of the record are not established fact at all.

Reasonable branches disagreed because they were pricing different things. The Executive priced risk of breakage, the Legislature priced the author's evenings, the Judiciary priced evidential standing. The verdict took the Legislature's ceiling on scope, the Executive's slicing method, and the Judiciary's filter on what counts as established.

Section fiveMinority opinion

The overruled position, at full strength

You are cutting slices three and four, and those contain the only defects on this record that are both audited and reader-harming today.

A keyboard user who opens the sign-in gate cannot tab out of it. Focus reaches the document body and lands on content sitting entirely behind a fixed overlay, with no visible ring and no signposted way back. That was measured in a real browser on the live build, and the auditor confirmed accounts.js has zero inert, zero aria-hidden, zero Tab keydown handler and one Escape listener. Focus drops to the body on every part change, across all eighteen routes, on both the contents links and the pager. The contents grid overflows at 320px, scrollWidth 347, measured independently by two workers. These cost 1.5 to 2 hours in total, and a site presented to hiring managers as a portfolio piece should not fail a keyboard user in the first interaction they attempt.

What would have to be true for it to win: that the author has the evenings. The Legislature made exactly that its own condition. If there is slack time to spend here, the five-slice sequence is better than two sittings and should be run instead.

The Comptroller has since found this dissent was softened. The focus-drop item appears in neither sitting despite the ruling claiming most of the dissent was kept. See the audit record below.

Section sixWhat the cross-check found

Six departments each claimed the "largest reader effect per hour" for a different item, and no two shared a denominator. Six local maxima were being sold as one global one. That became visible only when a reviewer read the reports side by side.

The same reviewer produced the total nobody had: 51.8 to 58.3 hours, of which ship-now alone is 13.35 to 15.85 and about twenty separate edits to one 349KB file with no tests. That is not one deploy. It is a month.

The framing reviewer caught the shared incentive: every department found things to add, and not one proposed cutting anything, because gaps are findable and excess is not. The practitioner caught that nobody costed the deploy itself; every report's clock stops at "the edit is made", not "the live page was checked and still works", on a site where deploying means dragging a zip with no version control and no rollback copy.

The omission reviewer caught the largest gap. Nobody reviewed the site as software that authenticates strangers. The account layer signs readers up, signs them in, resets passwords and stores progress, all from the browser. Six reports touched that surface and all walked past it. The author is applying for cyber security roles and hands hiring managers this URL, which is what makes it the finding that outranks every line-length item on the record.

Redacted The specific enumeration of that surface is withheld from this published copy while the finding is unremediated: as of publication nobody has reviewed it, so printing the list would amount to a to-do list against a live site. It is restored once the review has happened. This is a speed bump rather than a wall, and saying so is part of the point: the site’s script is served publicly. What is withheld is the convenience, and the confirmation that nobody has checked. See ADR-0012.

The future-reader lens produced the one nobody else reached: the guide has no way to fail loudly and no way to end. There is no analytics by design, so a broken font host, a dead form or a year of drift are all equally silent. Not one report proposed the cheap honest move of a dated line saying when the guide was last maintained.

What the researchers threw out, and why

The rejects are often the most useful part of a run. A pricing claim from an aggregator site stating that promotional model pricing would rise on 1 September 2026 was rejected because Anthropic's own release note of 10 August 2026 states the increase will not occur: no stated method, contradicted by primary. A roadmap page at a review site was rejected unread for having no traceable origin. Popup and interstitial conversion statistics were rejected because every traceable figure originated with companies selling popups. Accessibility vendor statistics of the form "X% of sites fail" were rejected because the publisher sells the remedy. A design-tool vendor's "50 to 75 character rule" was rejected on the same grounds.

Two rejections are worth more than the rest. The most-repeated line-length rule in web writing, "45 to 75 characters, 66 ideal", traces to Bringhurst asserting it in 1992 and was never tested; the researcher could not retrieve the book and every online statement of it was one site quoting another, so it was not cited and the 66-character figure is unverified. And a worker discarded its own first-pass summary of the live site after finding it had fabricated a per-part date table the page does not contain. That discard is why the run's claims rest on raw retrieved source rather than on summaries.

One interested party published against its own interest and that is unusually strong evidence: the accessibility scanning vendor whose survey was used states plainly that not all conformance failures can be detected automatically, which cuts against the product it sells.

Verdict

NOT SOUND

What the ruling rests on that it should not

The new evidence is wrong, and I checked it. I fetched the live page (348,810 bytes) and diffed it against G:\My Drive\ClaudeHub\projects\claude-guide\site\index.html (348,934). 57 diff lines, correct. Everything else is not. It is thirteen REDESIGN.md to docs/REDESIGN.md edits (65 bytes), not "nineteen or so", and the remaining 59 bytes are a markup change at line 5775: local reads <form class="say" name="feedback" method="POST" data-netlify="true" netlify-honeypot="company" id="sayForm"> against live's unregistered tag. So "57 diff lines, all comments. No functional, markup or script difference" is false, and the one item it misses is the very fix the ruling schedules in sitting one. Registering the form is already written; it needs deploying, not authoring.

Three further reliances. "Silently discarding messages" is stated as fact; Department 6 declared it unverified. Department 2's dropped Part 05 memory-redesign obsolescence is a wrong-class item the audit flagged and the ruling neither adjudicates nor discloses, while ruling "two exceptions". Department 5's SC 1.1.1 Level A item (0.2h, confirmed) is dropped unmentioned.

On what the Speaker reasoned itself

Three declared, one undeclared. First fails as above; the diff settles artefact identity but not the sceptic's viewport prong, which no receipt covers. Second: "roughly seven hours" does not sum. The ruling's own items at departmental hours are 11.85 to 14.85. Reasoning written as arithmetic. Third: "what a stranger feels within ten seconds" has no support. Dyson and Haselgrove favour 55 characters per line for comprehension; Shaikh and Chaparro found 95 characters per line read faster with no comprehension loss. Undeclared: the rescue of Department 3's "not generated" conclusion, which the Judiciary held unestablished for want of label and falsifier.

Section nineThe first move

Today, before touching anything: download the currently-live page from the site, save it where you will find it in six months, and put the date in the filename. Fifteen minutes.

It is not the closing deadline. The social image has the only external clock on this record, because the caches at Facebook, LinkedIn, X and Slack take days to weeks to re-scrape, and some need a manual re-scrape. That is a footnote here, not the lead.

The reason this goes first is that there is no rollback copy of the site anywhere. The deploy is a person dragging a zip, there is no version control pushing to the host, and the local build is one build ahead of live. The next deploy overwrites production with something nobody has compared against a saved copy.

Then, in the same evening, deploy the build you already have. The Comptroller established that the feedback form registration is written locally and undeployed, so the contact route is fixed on disk and broken in production. That is the cheapest repair on this record and it is already done.
